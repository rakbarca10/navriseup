80G Tax Exemption Guide: How Donating to an NGO Saves Tax in India

Published by: Navriseup India Care Research Team

Philanthropy in India offers dual benefits: creating sustainable social impact for marginalized communities while providing legitimate tax relief under Indian tax legislation. Under Section 80G of the Income Tax Act, 1961, individual taxpayers, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and corporations can claim deductions on eligible charitable contributions.

What is Section 80G of the Income Tax Act?

Section 80G is a provision under Chapter VI-A of the Indian Income Tax Act that allows eligible taxpayers to deduct charitable donations made to approved non-profit organizations, charitable trusts, and Section 8 companies from their gross total income before calculating taxable income.

To qualify, the non-profit organization must hold a valid 80G registration issued by the Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemptions). For instance, Navriseup India Care Foundation is registered under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, with active 12A and 80G certifications (URN: AAJCN0245LF2023101), enabling donors to claim lawful tax deductions.

How Much Deduction Can You Claim Under Section 80G?

Donations generally fall into two categories:

  • 100% Deduction Without Qualifying Limit: Applicable to specific government relief funds like the PM National Relief Fund.
  • 50% Deduction With or Without Qualifying Limit: Most non-governmental charitable organizations, including Navriseup India Care Foundation, fall under the 50% deduction category subject to a qualifying limit of 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI).

Step-by-Step Calculation Example

Suppose your Gross Total Income for the financial year is ₹12,00,000, and you donate ₹50,000 to an 80G-certified NGO for child education initiatives:

  1. Eligible deduction under 50% limit: ₹50,000 × 50% = ₹25,000.
  2. Adjusted Taxable Income becomes ₹12,00,000 − ₹25,000 = ₹11,75,000.
  3. Based on your tax slab, your net income tax liability is reduced proportionally, saving thousands of rupees while supporting critical humanitarian relief.

Essential Documents Required to Claim 80G Deductions

To successfully claim deductions when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR):

  • Valid Donation Receipt: Stating donor's name, PAN, donation amount, receipt number, and the NGO's official 80G Unique Registration Number (URN).
  • Form 10BE (Certificate of Donation): As mandated by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), NGOs upload donation records directly to the Income Tax portal and issue Form 10BE to donors.
  • NGO Legal Credentials: Official registration proof, such as Section 8 Incorporation and 12A/80G orders, accessible via our Legal Information Page.

Mode of Payment Guidelines

Cash donations exceeding ₹2,000 are ineligible for 80G tax deductions. To ensure tax compliance, contributions must be made through transparent banking channels, such as UPI, Net Banking, Debit/Credit Cards, or verified payment gateways.
